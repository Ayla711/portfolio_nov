<CaseSection id="overview" kind="overview">
  LingGou EduBot is a modular educational robot developed for hands-on STEAM
  learning at home and in school. The team combined flexible robotics,
  education expertise, and business strategy; I served as product manager and
  owned the path from opportunity framing to product definition.

<RoleBreakdown>
  I synthesized the research, mapped stakeholders, translated learning needs
  into product requirements, defined the MVP with the engineering team, and
  supported a longer-term ecosystem roadmap.
</RoleBreakdown>

The project focused on children aged 7–10 and proposed snap-fit modules with
built-in power and communication, allowing expressive structures without
wiring or technical setup.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="insight" kind="insight">
  A questionnaire with 205 Beijing parents found strong interest in technology
  and robotics, while observation of a coding workshop showed a gap between
  enthusiasm and the rigidity of available learning tools. The primary users
  were children, but adoption depended equally on parents and educators.

  <InsightQuote>
    The product had to balance three needs at once: creative independence for
    children, clear learning value for educators, and safety and trust for
    parents.
  </InsightQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="decisions" kind="decisions" layout="wide">
  I positioned LingGou between simple robot toys and curriculum-heavy coding
  kits. Four principles guided the strategy: a low barrier to start, open-ended
  modularity, expressive feedback, and parent-approved safety.

  explore the robot’s movement and modular configuration. The physical concept
  and product strategy were advanced together so the learning promise remained
  connected to engineering feasibility.

<CaseSection id="reflection" kind="reflection">
  The project received 100,000 CNY in national innovation funding and a Gold
  Award at the 2024 Challenge Cup. More important for my practice, it showed
  how product language can connect technical constraints, educational value,
  and business adoption across disciplines.

  <ReflectionBlock title="A shared problem definition was the team’s most important interface.">
    A future release would need longitudinal classroom testing, clearer measures
    of learning progress, and a smaller first module set. The broader community
    and content ecosystem should follow evidence of repeated engagement—not
    precede it.
  </ReflectionBlock>
</CaseSection>
