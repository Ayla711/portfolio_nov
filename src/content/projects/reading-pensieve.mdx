<CaseSection id="overview" kind="overview">
  The Reading Pensieve asks whether emerging interfaces can support deep
  reading without turning the book into another screen. The concept overlays
  optional notes, bookmarks, and memory cues around a paper book, then uses
  spaced review to help readers revisit what mattered.

  <RoleBreakdown>
    I conducted the reader interviews, framed the experience principles,
    designed the interaction story and compact device concept, and produced the
    simulated bookstore prototype.
  </RoleBreakdown>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="insight" kind="insight">
  Four reader interviews showed that paper’s value was not nostalgia alone. It
  supported attention, tangibility, long-term memory, and critical thought.
  Readers still wanted portability, flexible interaction, and easier ways to
  return to meaningful passages.

  <InsightQuote>
    The digital layer should arrive only when it helps the reader remember or
    reflect—and disappear when it competes with the page.
  </InsightQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="decisions" kind="decisions" layout="wide">
  I kept the physical book as the primary object and organized the concept
  around gesture rather than a permanent screen. A compact projector and sensor
  would recognize the book area, support lightweight annotation, and sync saved
  material for later review. Holographic laser projection remains a speculative
  future form factor, not a solved implementation.

<CaseSection id="prototype" kind="prototype" layout="wide">
  The bookstore prototype used video compositing to make the spatial behavior
  visible: a reader opens a paper book, summons supporting material with a
  gesture, and dismisses the layer to continue reading. This tested the rhythm
  of the experience, not the feasibility of a holographic display.

<CaseSection id="reflection" kind="reflection">
  The concept is strongest as a critique of attention-heavy reading tools and
  weakest where it depends on future projection hardware. A functional next
  step would use an available tabletop projector to test gesture discoverability,
  annotation accuracy, and whether review prompts genuinely support recall.

  <ReflectionBlock title="Emerging technology should protect the ritual before it adds spectacle.">
    Simplifying the project made the core proposition clearer: the system is not
    a digital replacement for the book. It is a temporary memory layer that the
    reader can invite and dismiss.
  </ReflectionBlock>
</CaseSection>
