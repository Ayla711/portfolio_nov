<CaseSection id="overview" kind="overview">
  PhageX explores how a safety system could interrupt a pig-butchering scam
  before emotional dependence becomes financial loss. I focused on young adults
  aged 25–35 and treated the work as a service and interaction-design problem,
  not only a detection problem.

<RoleBreakdown>
  I planned and synthesized the research, framed the intervention strategy,
  designed the glasses and mobile experience, created the visual system, and ran
  the immersive prototype test.
</RoleBreakdown>

The outcome is a dual-platform concept: smart glasses surface risk in the
moment, while a paired phone can archive evidence, notify trusted contacts,
and execute a high-risk lock protocol.

</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="insight" kind="insight" layout="wide">
  A 50-minute focus group with five digital-native professionals showed that
  scam awareness was high, yet direct or close-contact impact was still common.
  Mapping the full journey revealed that the harm develops through repeated
  trust-building rather than a single suspicious transaction.

<InsightQuote>
  The intervention cannot wait for the payment screen. It has to interrupt the
  emotional script—and appear outside the mobile channel already controlled by
  the scammer.
</InsightQuote>

<CaseSection id="decisions" kind="decisions" layout="wide">
  I compared a smart charger, a gamified phone app, and smart glasses. The
  glasses direction offered a first-person viewpoint, real-time response, and
  an intervention layer outside the compromised phone. That advantage came
  with clear constraints: battery life, false positives, public-camera trust,
  and the need to keep sensitive analysis on-device.

  post-production. This made the first-person timing testable without claiming
  a working detection model or production smart-glasses hardware.

<CaseSection id="reflection" kind="reflection">
  Participants found the simulated experience realistic and practical. They
  also wanted the guardian to remain visible and questioned continuous camera
  use in public. Those reactions moved privacy and system transparency from
  secondary concerns into core experience requirements.

  <ReflectionBlock title="The next iteration must earn the right to intervene.">
    A stronger prototype would test warning thresholds, false-positive recovery,
    user-controlled sensing states, and lighter hardware. PhageX currently
    validates an interaction strategy—not detection accuracy or technical
    feasibility.
  </ReflectionBlock>
</CaseSection>
