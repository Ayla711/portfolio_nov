<CaseSection id="overview" kind="overview">
  Tutela explores how a wearable device could support teenagers living with
  irritable bowel syndrome without making a private condition more visible. The
  concept combines stress-related sensing with heat and pneumatic relief in a
  discreet abdominal patch connected to a smartwatch.

  <RoleBreakdown>
    I conducted the user and expert research, translated the findings into the
    product concept and interaction flow, and prototyped the sensing and
    pneumatic behavior with Arduino, EMG components, and TPU fabrication.
  </RoleBreakdown>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="insight" kind="insight">
  The central design requirement was discretion. Support needed to work under
  clothing, fit everyday school routines, and give the wearer control over when
  relief or alerts appeared instead of publicly signaling a health condition.

  <InsightQuote>
    A health wearable can only feel supportive when the user controls its
    visibility, timing, and intensity.
  </InsightQuote>
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="decisions" kind="decisions" layout="wide">
  I organized the system around a lightweight adhesive patch and a connected
  smartwatch. The patch combines sensing, heating, air passages, and pneumatic
  chambers, while the watch handles status feedback and adjustable alert
  thresholds. Replaceable scent pads were explored as an optional calming cue,
  not a required part of the core interaction.
</CaseSection>

<CaseSection id="prototype" kind="prototype" layout="wide">
  The physical prototype was used to examine component placement, pneumatic
  behavior, and how the form could sit beneath clothing. Early user feedback
  highlighted two practical risks: security during movement and differences in
  heat sensitivity. These findings led to a skin-safe adhesive or support band
  and personalized thresholds as the next design priorities.

<CaseSection id="reflection" kind="reflection">
  The project demonstrates a tangible interaction direction, but it does not
  yet establish reliable long-term sensing or wear. The next step would be an
  extended study of comfort, attachment, personalized triggering, and safety
  before treating the concept as a real health product.

  <ReflectionBlock title="Discretion is part of the function, not a cosmetic layer.">
    The strongest outcome was a clearer product principle: relief, sensing, and
    feedback must remain adjustable and private if the wearable is expected to
    fit into a teenager’s daily life.
  </ReflectionBlock>
</CaseSection>
